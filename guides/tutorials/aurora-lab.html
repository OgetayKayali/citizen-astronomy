Open Aurora & Magnetosphere Lab

  1. Scroll down to Simulations. Click Aurora & Magnetosphere Lab (1), the card showing Earth’s magnetic field.

A moving view of Earth appears. The controls run down the right side. The time slider and a graph sit below Earth.

Open the lab and control time / Step 02

Pause and find Earth’s magnetic boundary

Start here Open the lab. It starts playing the southward storm scenario.

  1. Click Pause (1) below the scene. If the button already says Play, the clock is paused.
  2. Find Earth near the middle of the scene. The cyan boundary facing the +X · SUN direction is the magnetopause, where the solar wind meets the region dominated by Earth’s magnetic field.
What you should see

The button now reads Play. The upper-left readout shows Paused next to the time in model minutes. You can examine the scene while the clock holds still.

Open the lab and control time / Step 03

Play, change speed, or return to the start

Start here The lab is open. These controls are beneath the scene.

  1. Click Play (1) to advance the model. The same button changes to Pause. Click it again to hold the current time.
  2. To replay the event from its beginning, click Reset (2). The clock returns to zero and pauses.
  3. To watch faster, open the speed menu on the far right (3), choose 2× or 4×, then click Play. Choose 1× for the original pace.
What you should see

At 1×, one second of playback advances one model minute. The vertical line on the graph follows the clock.

Open the lab and control time / Step 04

Set the clock to 45 model minutes

Start here The lab is open. For this comparison use the time slider directly beneath the scene.

  1. Drag the slider handle (1) to just over a quarter of the way across the default 160-minute timeline. Dragging the handle pauses playback.
  2. Watch the last line of the upper-left readout (2) as you drag. Stop close to 045.0 model min. A few tenths of a minute either way will give nearly the same readings here.
What you should see

The readout shows roughly 045.0 model min · Paused. Leave the slider there as you change scenarios so each picture shows the same stage of the storm.

Frame the comparison →Read the quiet reference →Inspect the southward storm → Browse all steps

Compare three solar-wind scenarios / Step 06

Measure the boundary in quiet solar wind

Start here Pause near 45 model minutes. The Pressure comparison camera matches the example image.

  1. Scroll to the top of the right panel. Open SCENARIO (1) and choose Quiet Solar Wind. Wait for Scenario ready.
  2. In the readout (2), find Pressure and the line beginning MP. MP is the distance to the Sun-facing tip of the magnetopause. BS is the distance to the bow shock farther upstream. Both are in Earth radii, written R_E.
What you should see

The near-Earth pressure is about 1.34 nPa. The magnetopause lies 10.97 Earth radii from Earth’s center and the bow shock lies about 14.37 Earth radii out. These are the quiet reference distances.

Compare the northward storm →Compare the southward storm →Read the history graph → Browse all steps

Compare three solar-wind scenarios / Step 07

Compress the boundary with a northward storm

Start here With the lab open, pause near 45 minutes. Choose the Pressure comparison camera for the same framing as the screenshot.

  1. At the top right, open SCENARIO (1) and choose CME — Northward IMF. A CME is a coronal mass ejection, an outflow of plasma from the Sun. IMF is the magnetic field carried by the solar wind.
  2. Read the +24 R_E and MP lines (2). The wind at the near-Earth input travels at 800 km/s and its northward field component, Bz, is +15 nT.
What you should see

The pressure rises to about 19.27 nPa and the magnetopause moves inward to 7.35 Earth radii, compared with 10.97 in quiet wind. The bow-shock estimate here extends beyond the pressure range recommended for that model, as the amber note explains.

Compare three solar-wind scenarios / Step 08

Reverse the field at the same pressure

Start here Pause around 45 minutes. Use the Pressure comparison camera to match the screenshots.

  1. Open SCENARIO (1) and choose CME — Southward IMF. Wait for Scenario ready. The wind speed and density match the northward storm.
  2. Read Bz, Pressure, and MP in the readout (2). Use the two buttons above the tutorial image to compare the captured southward and northward cases without moving the camera.
What you should see

Bz has changed from +15 to −15 nT. Pressure stays near 19.27 nPa in both storms. The modeled magnetopause moves from 7.35 to 5.83 Earth radii. The bow-shock estimate stays near 9.56 because this model uses wind pressure to place it.

Trace a storm and change its inputs / Step 09

Find the delay between the two pressure readings

Start here Open the lab. At the top of the right panel choose CME — Southward IMF under SCENARIO. If you have edited this scenario, select Quiet Solar Wind, then reselect the southward storm to restore its defaults.

  1. Drag the timeline handle (1) until the readout (2) is close to 013.0 model min. Read the two numbers on the Pressure line from left to right.
  2. Find the white vertical cursor on the history graph (3). The dashed gold curve is pressure at the upstream monitor. The solid gold curve is pressure at the input closer to Earth. Read both against the left scale in nPa.
  3. Follow the cyan magnetopause and violet bow-shock curves using the right-hand scale in Earth radii. Drag the handle a little farther right to watch when these distances begin to fall.
What you should see

Near minute 13, the upstream monitor already feels the storm. In the screenshot its pressure is 8.28 nPa, while the near-Earth input is still at 1.34 nPa. The rise in the dashed gold curve comes first.

Jump to the storm plateau →Follow the later recovery →Replay the arrival slowly → Browse all steps

Trace a storm and change its inputs / Step 10

Read the tail’s memory after the wind recovers

Start here Use the default southward CME. If you edited its wind settings, switch to Quiet Solar Wind, then back to CME — Southward IMF.

  1. Drag the time handle (1) until the clock shows about 100 model min.
  2. Scroll the right panel to Coupling & tail memory and expand that section if needed. Read Dayside driver C and Stored-flux proxy F together (2). C represents how strongly the wind is driving the model now. F tracks how much of the earlier disturbance the model still retains.
What you should see

At minute 100 the driver reads 0.00, but F is still about 0.220 and release is positive. The model’s tail takes time to relax after the wind has recovered.

Go back to the arrival →Connect this result to aurora → Browse all steps

Trace a storm and change its inputs / Step 11

Change wind density and read the pressure

Start here Select the default southward CME and pause around 45 minutes. At the top of the right panel, expand Solar wind · event plateau.

  1. Leave Speed (km/s) at 800. Double-click the value in Proton density (cm⁻³) (1), type 10, and press Enter.
  2. Read Calculated plateau pressure (2). Wait for Scenario ready while CAst recalculates the history for the whole event.
  3. To recover the original storm settings, choose Quiet Solar Wind and then CME — Southward IMF from the scenario menu.
What you should see

At 800 km/s, lowering density from 18 to 10 lowers the calculated plateau pressure from about 19.27 to 10.70 nPa. The screenshot shows the edited value before restoring the preset.

Explore the scene and the aurora connection / Step 12

Orbit, pan, or zoom around Earth

Start here Open the lab. To match this close view, scroll to Field & scene → Camera preset and choose Earth.

  1. Press and hold the left mouse button inside the outlined scene area (1). Move the pointer sideways, then release to orbit Earth.
  2. To move the picture sideways or vertically, hold the right mouse button and drag. Roll the mouse wheel over the scene to move closer or farther away.
  3. To recover a familiar view, select Dayside or Pressure comparison from Camera preset.
What you should see

The viewpoint moves around Earth while the clock and wind settings stay at their selected values if playback is paused.

Explore the scene and the aurora connection / Step 13

Trace closed, open, and interplanetary field lines

Start here Open the lab. The field lines are easiest to follow with the southward CME paused.

  1. Scroll down the right-hand controls to Field & scene. Open Camera preset (1) and select Topology · schematic.
  2. Follow a cyan curve back to Earth at both ends. Then look for a pink curve with one end on Earth and a gold interplanetary line passing by Earth.
What you should see

The preset enables all three line types. Their colors distinguish how they connect. The pink curves illustrate possible open connections, so counting them cannot tell you how much magnetic flux has opened.

Explore the scene and the aurora connection / Step 14

Hide particles and isolate the magnetic lines

Start here Open the lab. Choose the topology view for the example. Scroll the right panel to the checkboxes under Field & scene.

  1. Clear Solar-wind plasma · gold (1) to remove the moving particles. The gold interplanetary field curves are controlled separately.
  2. Clear IMF curves & Bz arrows · gold (2) to leave the cyan and pink field lines easier to follow. Click either checkbox again to restore that layer.
What you should see

Each checkbox hides or restores its own layer. The modeled pressure and boundary distances remain the same at a fixed time. Both controls are checked in the screenshot so you can find them before starting.

Read the remaining lines →Change the scene detail → Browse all steps

Explore the scene and the aurora connection / Step 15

Change the number of field lines and particles

Start here Open the lab and expand Field & scene in the right panel. Scroll just past the layer checkboxes.

  1. Open the field-line menu (1), shown as 48 field lines, and select 24 field lines to draw fewer geomagnetic lines.
  2. Open the plasma-seed menu (2), shown as Balanced · 1600 plasma seeds, and select Low · 500 plasma seeds to draw fewer particles. Restore either setting from the same menu.
What you should see

The view has fewer drawn elements. While paused, the pressure, boundary distances, and model time keep their values.

Explore the scene and the aurora connection / Step 16

Find what the model calculates

Start here The lab is open. Scroll near the bottom of the right-hand controls.

  1. Click Model & interpretation (1) to expand it. Scroll inside the panel to read the notes.
  2. Find Empirical equilibria. The lab calculates target boundary positions from formulas fitted to observations. The displayed boundaries take time to move toward those targets.
What you should see

The panel explains the calculated pressure, the fitted boundaries, and the schematic field lines. It also explains why extreme wind settings can extend the bow-shock estimate beyond the range used to fit it.

Read boundary distances in a scenario →Check what the lab can say about aurora → Browse all steps

Explore the scene and the aurora connection / Step 17

Find the connection to visible aurora

Start here Open the lab. If you want to see the model’s tail response first, open the recovery example.

  1. Scroll to the bottom of the right-hand controls. Click Connection to future aurora (1) to expand its note.
  2. Read the note beneath the heading. The field and tail readouts show part of the chain of events that can lead to aurora. Predicting an oval or its brightness also requires a model for the energy and location of particles entering the atmosphere.
What you should see

The current lab displays Earth’s magnetic response. Aurora brightness and location are outside its calculations, so the lesson ends with the field and tail readouts.
