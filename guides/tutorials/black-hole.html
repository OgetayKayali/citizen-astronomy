Open the light lab / Step 01

Open Black Hole

Start here Start the Windows version of Citizen Astronomy (CAst). The simulation supplies the gas and sky.

  1. Scroll down the mode picker to Simulations. Click the Black Hole card (1).
  2. If you already have a workspace open, choose Mode → Black Hole from the top menu.
What you should see

Controls appear on the left and the simulated sky appears on the right. A Preparing fluid history… percentage may appear while CAst calculates the gas disk’s earlier motion.

Find the disk and pause it →Choose a spinning or nonspinning hole →Save a picture → Browse all steps

Open the light lab / Step 02

Find the disk and pause the gas

Start here Open Black Hole. If the left controls are hidden, click in the view and press F1.

  1. Wait for the preparation percentage to disappear. Look for the thin band of bright gas across the center and the arc above it. Light from the far side of the same disk bends around the hole to reach you.
  2. At the top of the left panel, click Pause (1). Click Play in the same spot when you want the gas to move again.
What you should see

The button changes to Play when paused. You can still move the camera and inspect the same moment from another angle.

Move around the paused disk →See where the gas is concentrated →See how light bends →Save this view → Browse all steps

Choose your viewpoint / Step 03

Orbit with the mouse

Start here Open the lab. For a steady comparison, pause the gas. If a movie preview is on, clear Preview shot under Video export.

  1. Place the pointer in the marked area of the simulation (1). Hold the left mouse button, drag up or down a short distance, and release. Drag left or right to orbit around the other way.
  2. Roll the mouse wheel over the simulation one notch at a time to move closer or farther away.
What you should see

The band opens into a broader disk as you approach a face-on view. The wheel changes how much of the window the disk fills.

Set the angle with a slider →Change the background sky →Save this angle → Browse all steps

Choose your viewpoint / Step 04

Set a camera angle you can repeat

Start here Open the lab. If Preview shot is checked under Video export, clear it to control the camera.

  1. Click Camera (1) in the left panel to expand it. Drag Inclination (2) toward the left for a view from above the disk. Try the middle of the slider (90°) to look along its edge.
  2. Drag Distance (3) a little to the right to see more of the surrounding sky. The value beside each slider records your setting.
What you should see

The disk’s apparent tilt follows the inclination setting. Distance is measured in gravitational radii (rg), the length scale set by the black hole’s mass.

Reveal the bending of background light →Plan a camera move →Save the current angle → Browse all steps

Experiment with the black hole and its light / Step 05

Switch to a nonspinning black hole

Start here Open the lab. Scroll to the top of the left panel.

  1. Click Spacetime (1) and choose Schwarzschild. This selects a black hole with zero spin.
  2. Read the Horizon and ISCO values (2). The horizon marks the boundary beyond which light cannot escape. ISCO is the radius of the innermost stable circular orbit.
What you should see

The Spin slider disappears. The readout gives a horizon of 2.000 rg and an ISCO of 6.000 rg. Wait for gas preparation to finish if the percentage reappears.

Compare with a rotating hole →Examine the warped grid → Browse all steps

Experiment with the black hole and its light / Step 06

Change a rotating black hole’s spin

Start here At the top of the Black Hole panel, set Spacetime to Kerr (rotating).

  1. Drag Spin (1) a little to the right and read the new spin value. Watch the Horizon and ISCO line below the slider as you change it.
  2. Click Cinematic 0.6 (2) to return to a spin of 0.60 with cinematic colors. Let the gas preparation finish.
What you should see

For this disk, gas orbits in the same direction as the black hole spins. Increasing spin moves the innermost stable circular orbit inward. At 0.60 the readout is about 3.829 rg for the ISCO.

Check the zero-spin reference →Explore brightness and color →Inspect the gas → Browse all steps

Experiment with the black hole and its light / Step 07

Compare the two brightness treatments

Start here Open the lab. Use the default disk, or set Disk view to Emission. Pause to hold the gas still.

  1. Look at the two sides of the disk. At the top of the panel, check Physical appearance (1). Compare the brightness on each side while you keep the same viewpoint.
  2. Clear Physical appearance to return to the cinematic colors. You can repeat the switch while the gas stays paused.
What you should see

In this angled view one side becomes much brighter. Motion of the emitting gas and gravitational shifts affect the brightness in the physical rendering. The display colors are illustrative.

Try the comparison at another angle →Inspect different gas properties →Save this appearance → Browse all steps

Experiment with the black hole and its light / Step 08

See where the gas is concentrated

Start here Open the lab. Scroll inside the left panel to Disk & time. Click the Visual effects header to collapse it if needed.

  1. Expand Disk & time (1). Check Show disk and Evolving fluid (2). If a preparation percentage appears, wait for the disk to be ready.
  2. Click Disk view (3) and choose Surface density. Compare the pattern in the disk’s band and in its lensed arc. Try Temperature for the gas temperature or Flow tracers to follow patterns as the gas moves.
  3. Choose Emission in the same menu to return to the luminous disk.
What you should see

Each selection displays a different property of the same gas. Surface density describes how much gas lies in a patch of disk, measured through its thickness.

Compare emission brightness →Trace background light →Save a disk map → Browse all steps

Experiment with the black hole and its light / Step 09

See gravitational lensing with a grid

Start here Open the lab. Scroll inside the left panel to Appearance & quality. Collapse longer sections by clicking their headers if needed.

  1. Expand Appearance & quality (1). Click Background (2) and choose Lensing grid.
  2. Follow a grid line from the outer part of the view toward the dark center. To restore the sky, choose Stars in the same menu.
What you should see

Lines that are nearly straight at the edge curve sharply near the hole. The grid reveals how the black hole bends light arriving from behind it.

Follow the grid from another angle →Adjust the Milky Way →Save the warped grid → Browse all steps

Experiment with the black hole and its light / Step 10

Change the Milky Way behind the disk

Start here Open the lab. If you see a grid or black background, set Background to Stars first.

  1. Expand Visual effects (1) if its controls are hidden. Check Milky Way background (2).
  2. Move Sky brightness (3) to the right to make the background easier to see. Watch a dark patch away from the bright disk as you adjust. Clear Milky Way background to try the generated star field.
What you should see

The brightness slider changes the panorama behind the disk. Its controls disappear when you switch the panorama off.

Switch to the lensing grid →Save the sky and disk → Browse all steps

Save a still or make a movie / Step 11

Save the current view as a picture

Start here Open the lab and frame a view you want to keep. Pause if you want to hold a particular moment.

  1. Scroll down the left control panel to the Save PNG... button (1), just above Video export. Clicking an expanded section heading collapses its controls and shortens the scroll.
  2. Click Save PNG....
What you should see

A Save PNG file picker opens. The saved picture contains the simulation viewport. When the Milky Way panorama is visible, its credit appears in the corner.

Choose the filename and save →Plan an animated shot → Browse all steps

Save a still or make a movie / Step 12

Name and check your PNG

Start here Click Save PNG... so the file picker is open.

  1. Choose a folder you can find again. Click File name (1) and enter my-black-hole.png.
  2. Click Save (2). Open that folder in File Explorer and double-click the PNG to view the picture.
What you should see

The folder contains my-black-hole.png and my-black-hole.png.txt. Open the text file to read the scene settings that went with your picture.

Save another camera angle →Animate the camera instead → Browse all steps

Save a still or make a movie / Step 13

Preview an orbit before exporting

Start here Open the lab and frame the disk. Scroll inside the left panel to Video export.

  1. Expand Video export (1) if its options are hidden. Click Camera preset (2) and choose Orbit. Sweep sets the angle the camera travels around the hole.
  2. Check Preview shot (3). Leave Play preview clear and drag Shot position (4) from left to right to inspect the path, or check Play preview to watch it run.
  3. Clear Preview shot when you want to position the camera manually again.
What you should see

The camera moves through the orbit while you drag the slider. The chosen camera preset remains selected for export.

Export a playable MP4 →Export individual PNG frames →Save a single picture → Browse all steps

Save a still or make a movie / Step 14

Export a playable MP4

Start here Open the lab. Scroll to Video export and expand it. Choose Current view to keep the camera still or preview an orbit.

  1. For a short first test, set Resolution to 720p, Frame rate to 24 fps, and drag Duration to its left end for one second.
  2. Open Format (1) and choose MP4 (H.264). Click Browse output... (2), choose a folder, enter black-hole-orbit.mp4, and confirm. Leaving the output path blank instead uses the CAst Black Hole folder inside Pictures.
  3. Click Export video / frames (3). Wait for the progress bar to finish and open the saved MP4 in a video player.
What you should see

The export status reports the saved file. Choosing Orbit moves the camera during the clip. Current view keeps the same framing while the gas evolves unless Freeze disk animation is checked.

Save the frames separately →Try a different camera path → Browse all steps

Save a still or make a movie / Step 15

Export numbered PNG frames

Start here Open the lab. Expand Video export and choose a camera preset. The orbit preview lets you inspect the path first.

  1. Set Resolution (1) to 720p and the Frame rate beneath it to 24 fps. Drag Duration (2) all the way left for one second.
  2. Set Format (3) to PNG sequence. Leave Output path blank to use Pictures → CAst Black Hole, or click Browse output... and choose a parent folder. CAst creates a new folder inside it.
  3. Click Export video / frames (4). Wait for the saved status, then open the output folder to step through the individual images.
What you should see

At 24 frames per second, a one-second sequence contains 24 PNG images. The export also records the camera and scene settings.

Make a single video file →Adjust the shot →Save just the current view → Browse all steps
